\section{Replication design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Solving a programme that is not quite a quadratic programme}
Equation~\eqref{eq:qp} is not a quadratic programme. The semi-continuous
condition, that a holding is either zero or at least five basis points, makes
it mixed-integer, and solving it exactly over five hundred names at
twenty-nine review dates is a different undertaking from the one the rest of
this paper is about. We use the approximation index providers themselves
describe: solve the continuous relaxation, drop the holdings that fall below
the floor, re-solve on the survivors, and repeat to a cap of fifteen passes.
The procedure terminates because each pass strictly shrinks the name set, and
in practice it converges in far fewer passes than the cap allows.

This is an approximation and we do not measure its cost. An exact mixed-integer
solve on a handful of dates would bound the optimality gap, and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} records its absence as a limitation rather than
presenting the heuristic as a solution.

Three timing details are reproduced from the methodology because getting them
wrong flatters the result. The covariance matrix is dated to the end of the
month preceding the review, not to the review date, so the May rebalance uses
an end-of-April matrix; using the review-date matrix is a month of hindsight
the index never had. Turnover is measured against the previous book after it
has drifted with prices, not against the previous optimisation target, because
the drifted book is what a manager would actually be trading away from.
Reviews are effective on the last business day of May and November.

\subsection{The relaxation ladder}
The constraint set in \eqref{eq:qp} is not always feasible, and the provider's
methodology prescribes what to do when it is not. The sequence is specific and
an intuitive ordering would get it wrong in two places. Turnover loosens first,
in five-point steps to a ceiling of thirty per cent. The minimum holding then
walks down a basis point at a time toward one. Only after both are exhausted
does the weight cap loosen, paired with the sector band. The style band is
never relaxed at any stage, so a portfolio remains within 0.25 standard
deviations of the parent on every constrained style whatever else gives way.
And when the whole sequence fails, the index is not rebalanced on loosened
terms; it is not rebalanced at all that review, which we reproduce by carrying
the previous weights forward unchanged. Appendix~\ref{app:ladder} lists the
thirteen rungs in order.

The distinction between a loosened bound and an abandoned review matters for
reading Section~\ref{sec:constraints}. A loosened bound still yields an
optimised book that satisfies the bound in force. An abandoned review yields no
trade, after which the carried-forward book drifts with prices and can end a
period further outside the sector band than the optimiser would ever have
placed it. The two are reported separately.

\subsection{The heuristic rule and the evaluation}
The heuristic index needs no such machinery. At each quarterly review we take
the hundred parent members with the lowest trailing 252-day realised
volatility and weight them by \eqref{eq:splv}. There is no covariance matrix
and no constraint to be infeasible.

Replicas are compared against the funds rather than against the indices,
because fund prices are free and index levels are not. Both are evaluated on
daily total returns, gross and net of a five basis point one-way cost applied
to turnover at each rebalance, and we report both.

The two replicas cannot begin on the same day, and the reason is itself part of
the answer. The heuristic needs one trailing volatility number per stock, so it
can be run from its first quarterly review after the fund launched in May 2011.
The optimiser needs a fitted factor covariance, which needs 104 weeks of weekly
factor returns, which given a January 2010 exposure start puts its first
replicated review in May 2012. A heuristic can be run the week it is described;
an optimiser cannot. Every table reports each replica from its own first
review and again over the window common to both, so no comparison between them
rests on their covering different periods.
