\section{Identifying the market factor}
\label{app:identification}
Industry membership in \eqref{eq:csreg} is exhaustive and exclusive, so the
industry dummies sum to a column of ones for every stock and the design matrix
is rank deficient by one. The market factor is not separately identified
without a side condition.

Let $c_t$ be the vector of industry capitalisation shares at $t$, summing to
one, and order the $K$ industries so that the largest share is last. Define
$R \in \mathbb{R}^{K\times(K-1)}$ by
\begin{equation}
 R = \begin{bmatrix} I_{K-1} \\[2pt]
     -c_1/c_K \ \ \cdots \ \ -c_{K-1}/c_K \end{bmatrix},
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
so that $c^{\top}R = 0$ by construction. Writing $f^{\rm ind} = Rg$ and fitting
\begin{equation}
 r_t = \alpha_t\ind + D_t R\,g_t + \Xsty_{t-1} f^{\rm sty}_t + u_t
 \label{eq:reparam}
\end{equation}
by weighted least squares in $(\alpha, g, f^{\rm sty})$ gives a full-rank
problem whose solution satisfies \eqref{eq:zerosum} exactly. Industry returns
are recovered as $Rg$. Placing the largest industry last means the division in
the final row of \eqref{eq:basis} is by the largest available denominator,
which keeps the reparametrisation numerically well behaved.

The alternative of dropping one industry column produces the same fitted values
and the same residuals, so a practitioner checking $R^2$ or specific returns
would see no difference. What changes is the meaning of every coefficient. With
a column dropped, the intercept is the omitted industry's return rather than
the market return, and each remaining industry coefficient is a difference
against the omitted one. A factor so defined cannot be compared with a
market factor from any model that identifies differently.

The repository's test suite checks three things about this construction, and
they are stated here because the correctness of \eqref{eq:csreg} is load-bearing
for the structural covariance estimator and therefore for
Table~\ref{tab:horserace}. First, that $c^{\top}R$ vanishes numerically, to
within $10^{-12}$. Second, that the fitted industry returns satisfy
\eqref{eq:zerosum} in the output rather than merely by construction, to within
$10^{-10}$. Third, that dropping a column reproduces the residuals to within
$10^{-8}$ while producing a demonstrably different intercept, which is the
evidence that the two approaches are not notational variants of one another.

Two further checks bear on whether the identified market factor means what the
name claims. Its weekly series correlates above 0.98 with the independently
rebuilt cap-weighted parent index, and its annualised mean differs from that
index's by less than three percentage points. Neither is a tight bound, and
they are reported as sanity checks rather than as validation of the factor
model.
