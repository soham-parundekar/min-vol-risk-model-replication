The heuristic replicates almost exactly. At 1.62\% annualised tracking error
and a correlation of 0.9939, the SPLV replica is doing what the fund does, and
the residual difference is attributable to the parent-universe proxy, the
rebalance-date approximation and trading frictions rather than to anything
about the rule. A replicator who reads the two sentences in the prospectus and
implements them has the index. This is the control case in the paper, and its
purpose is to establish that the pipeline around it works: the data, the
membership panel, the corporate-action handling and the evaluation machinery
are common to both replications, so a 1.62\% result on the simple rule bounds
how much of the harder rule's error can be blamed on infrastructure.

The optimised replication is a different matter. The structural specification
reaches 5.83\%, with a correlation of 0.9320 and, more tellingly, a beta to the
fund of 1.090 and a realised volatility of 15.73\% against the fund's 13.45\%.
The replica is not merely different from USMV; it is systematically riskier
than it. Something in the specification is permitting exposure the real index
does not take.

\subsection{The exemption, read two ways}
Section~\ref{sec:rules} identified one constraint the published document does
not pin down: which factor or factors the style exemption covers. The base
specification takes the literal reading and frees residual volatility alone,
leaving beta constrained inside the $\pm0.25$ band like every other style. The
variant frees beta as well.

The last row of Table~\ref{tab:replication} is that variant. Tracking error
falls from 5.83\% to 3.73\%, a reduction of 2.10 percentage points. Correlation
rises from 0.9320 to 0.9637. Realised volatility falls from 15.73\% to 13.97\%
against the fund's 13.45\%, and the beta to the fund moves from 1.090 to 1.001.

That last number is the one worth pausing on. A beta of 1.001 to the fund is
not a marginal improvement in fit; it means the variant replica and USMV are,
to a first approximation, taking the same amount of market risk, which the base
specification was not.

The mechanism is visible in the realised tilts, and it is not the one a reader
might expect. Under the base specification the median active beta across the
twenty-nine reviews is $-0.248$ against a band of $0.25$, with a minimum of
$-0.250$: beta is pinned against its bound at essentially every rebalance. The
optimiser wants more negative beta than it is allowed. Freeing the bound sends
the median active beta to $-0.718$, with a minimum of $-0.950$.

What does not happen is as informative. Residual volatility, the style the base
case already exempts, does not become a larger tilt once beta is freed; its
median moves from $-0.209$ to $-0.176$, slightly closer to the parent. The
optimiser is not being released to take more of the exposure it already had.
It is substituting one route to low volatility for a more direct one, and the
base specification had closed that route. A minimum-variance programme held to
the parent's beta is being denied the most direct means of reducing variance
there is, and it compensates with an exposure that does the job less
efficiently, which is why the base replica ends up both further from the fund
and more volatile than it.

We are not claiming the variant is the correct reading of the methodology. The
published document says what it says, and a replicator reading it literally
would implement the base case. The claim is narrower and, we think, more
useful: a single unresolved noun phrase in a constraint document is worth 2.10
percentage points of tracking error, and a reader of that document has no way
to tell which reading the index uses. Section~\ref{sec:estimator} puts that
magnitude in context by asking what the covariance estimator is worth.
